About Our Academy

Celebrating tradition, culture, and the art of Kuchipudi.

Our Story

Founded by Guru Smt. Haritha Kulakarni in the Atlanta area in 2014, Sri Natyalaya Kuchipudi Academy was established with a vision to preserve, promote, and pass on the rich tradition of Kuchipudi to future generations. Today, Sri Natyalaya proudly offers Kuchipudi training in Cumming, Canton, and Dawsonville, Georgia, bringing classical dance education to students and families across these communities. The academy provides systematic training in the essential elements of Kuchipudi, including adavus, jathis, rhythm, mudras, facial expressions, abhinaya, repertoire, and stage presentation. Beyond technique, students are encouraged to understand the stories, mythology, cultural values, and spiritual meaning embodied within each composition. At Sri Natyalaya, learning Kuchipudi is viewed as much more than learning steps and choreography. Classical dance is a journey that nurtures discipline, confidence, dedication, grace, creativity, and respect for Indian culture and tradition.

Students are given opportunities to showcase their learning through annual recitals, cultural celebrations, community events, and stage productions. These experiences help young dancers develop confidence, stage presence, teamwork, and a deeper appreciation for the art they practice. Sri Natyalaya also prepares dedicated students for SAMPADA examinations, providing structured training in both the practical and theoretical aspects of Kuchipudi and guiding students toward formal certification in classical dance. The academy is committed to providing a nurturing environment where every student is encouraged to discover their individual strengths while developing a strong foundation in Kuchipudi. Through patient instruction, personal attention, and meaningful performance opportunities, students are guided to grow not only as dancers but also as confident individuals. Over the years, Sri Natyalaya has grown beyond a dance academy into a vibrant artistic community where students and families come together through dance, devotion, discipline, culture, and tradition. Through Sri Natyalaya, the timeless beauty of Kuchipudi continues to flourish in a new generation of dancers while remaining deeply rooted in its classical heritage.

Meet Our Instructor

Your Teacher's Name

A Dream Transformed into Art

In Telugu, “కల” (dream) and “కళ” (art) sound alike. Haritha’s journey beautifully unites both—a childhood dream that grew into a lifelong journey in Kuchipudi. Inspired by her father, a talented Hindustani flute artist, Haritha developed a love for music and dance from an early age. She began learning dance at the age of seven, participating in school and cultural programs and earning recognition for her performances. After completing her 10th grade, Haritha moved to Hyderabad and stayed at a Kuchipudi dance academy to pursue intensive training. This marked an important turning point in her life, as dance became not simply an interest, but a disciplined pursuit. She had the privilege of training under the legendary Padma Shri Smt. Shobha Naidu, learning the technique, expression, grace, and traditions of Kuchipudi. She later trained under renowned dance artist Smt. Aruna Bhikshu and earned a Diploma in Kuchipudi Dance from Potti Sreeramulu Telugu University, Hyderabad, India. Haritha performed alongside her gurus in prominent cities across India and participated in numerous cultural competitions, receiving awards, honors, and recognition for her performances.

Her repertoire includes Kalyana Srinivasam, Chandalika, Sri Krishna Sharanam Mama, Bhama Kalapam, Sri Krishna Parijatham, and Vipranarayana. Through abhinaya, graceful movement, expressive gestures, and rhythmic footwork, Haritha brings the stories, emotions, and traditions of Kuchipudi to life on stage. Today, her journey has evolved from that of a student and performer to a guru nurturing the next generation of Kuchipudi dancers. Through Sri Natyalaya Kuchipudi Academy, established in 2013, she strives to give her students more than dance instruction. Her teaching emphasizes discipline, devotion, respect for the guru-shishya tradition, confidence, and a deeper connection to Indian culture and heritage. For Haritha, teaching Kuchipudi in America carries a special responsibility. Many of her students are growing up thousands of miles from the land where this classical art originated. Through dance, she hopes they not only master its movements and expressions, but also understand the stories, values, language, spirituality, and cultural roots carried within the art form. Each student who steps onto the stage represents a continuation of that tradition. Seeing young dancers embrace Kuchipudi with pride is one of the most meaningful chapters of Haritha’s journey and a reminder that classical art remains alive when it is learned, loved, practiced, and passed forward. Alongside her artistic journey, Haritha balances her roles as a mother and IT professional, bringing together family, career, community, and the classical art that has shaped her life. In her life, the dream became the art—and the art became a lifelong devotion.
